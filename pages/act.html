Move. Build. Connect.

Take Action

Everything here is free. Share it. Use it. Improve on it.

You don't have to burn the world down today. But you do have to start somewhere, and somewhere is always here, now, with the people around you.

Start or Join a Community Group

The most powerful political unit is the group of people who know each other, trust each other, and show up for each other. Building that group is the work.

  • Start with who you already know. Neighbors, coworkers, people from a place of worship, parents from a school. Existing relationships are faster to organize than cold outreach.
  • Host a first meeting. It doesn't need to be formal. A meal, a walk, a gathering. Find out what people need. Find out what people can offer.
  • Make a shared resource list. Who has tools? Who can drive? Who has first aid training? Who speaks multiple languages? This is your network's actual inventory.
  • Set a regular cadence. Monthly gatherings. A group chat. Something recurring. Consistency builds trust faster than intensity.
  • Start before you're ready. There is no perfect structure. A WhatsApp group of six neighbors is a mutual aid network. Begin.

Organizer Resources

Below are starting points. None of these are the final word — adapt everything to your context, your community, your constraints.

For Meeting Facilitation

Good meetings are run on consent, not authority. Use structured speaking (rounds, hands, etc.) so that the loudest people don't set the agenda. Document decisions in writing. Rotate facilitation so no single person becomes the de facto leader.

For Conflict Resolution

Conflict happens in any community. Having a process before conflict occurs is essential. Transformative justice frameworks focus on repairing harm rather than punishing individuals. Build agreements about how you'll handle disagreement before you need them.

For Fundraising Without Hierarchy

Mutual aid networks run on contributed resources. Be transparent about what comes in and what goes out. Use flat decision-making for spending. Avoid dependency on any single donor. The goal is resilience, not scale.

For Growing Carefully

Scale changes culture. A group of 8 works differently than a group of 80. Don't grow faster than your relationships can support. Depth of trust matters more than numbers. A tight, functional network of 12 does more than a sprawling, dysfunctional one of 200.

Digital Security Basics

Community organizing attracts attention from people who want to stop it. Protect yourself and your people.

  • Use Signal for sensitive communications. It's free, open-source, and end-to-end encrypted.
  • Use strong, unique passwords and a password manager (Bitwarden is free and open-source).
  • Be thoughtful about what you document and where. Public platforms have different risks than private channels.
  • Know your rights. In most jurisdictions, you don't have to answer police questions. Knowing what your legal rights are is not paranoia — it's preparation.

Further Reading

Theory is useful. It helps you understand patterns, learn from history, and explain to others why things work the way they do. But it follows practice, it doesn't replace it.

  • Mutual Aid: Building Solidarity During This Crisis (and the Next) — Dean Spade
  • The Anarchist Cookbook of Mutual Aid — various, public domain
  • Emergent Strategy — adrienne maree brown
  • The Dispossessed — Ursula K. Le Guin (fiction that illuminates everything)
  • Debt: The First 5,000 Years — David Graeber
  • Historical accounts of the Spanish Civil War anarchist collectives, the Black Panther Party's survival programs, and COVID-19 mutual aid networks globally.
