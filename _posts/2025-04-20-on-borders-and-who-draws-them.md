Lines on maps do not occur in nature. They are drawn by people with interests, and those interests have almost never been the interests of the people living where the lines are drawn.

The border between the United States and Mexico runs through the middle of land that belonged to Indigenous peoples for thousands of years, was claimed by Spain, transferred to Mexico, and seized by the United States in 1848. The families on either side of that line in 1848 did not suddenly become different kinds of people. They became different kinds of documents.

## What Borders Actually Do

Borders serve several functions, almost all of them upward-redistributive:

**They control labor supply.** A worker who cannot legally move is a worker who cannot leave a bad employer, a dangerous job, or a low wage. Immigration restrictions are fundamentally a tool of labor control. The same corporations that lobby for restricted immigration also lobby for the right to move capital, goods, and data freely across those same borders. The asymmetry is not accidental.

**They create legal status hierarchies.** The undocumented worker is not just a worker without papers. They are a worker without rights, without recourse, without the ability to organize or complain or leave without risk of deportation. This condition is profitable for employers. It was designed to be.

**They manufacture loyalty to national identity over class identity.** The French factory worker and the German factory worker have far more in common with each other than either has with the French or German factory owner. Nationalism exists in part to prevent that recognition. If workers organized across national lines as consistently as capital operates across national lines, the balance of power would shift immediately.

## The People at the Border

Go to any border crossing in the world and watch what happens. Wealthy people with the right passports flow through in minutes. Poor people, darker people, people with the wrong documents, are held, questioned, detained, turned around.

This is not a security system. It is a sorting mechanism. It sorts the world into people whose movement is valued and people whose movement is controlled.

The people moving across those borders are, overwhelmingly, people who are fleeing the consequences of decisions made by exactly the kind of people those borders protect.

## Solidarity Across Lines

This is not an argument that borders should disappear tomorrow or that the political difficulties of open migration don't exist. They do. But it is an argument that the framework — "those people coming here" as threat rather than as neighbors we haven't met yet — is a manufactured frame that serves specific interests.

When the Somali refugee and the unemployed worker in a de-industrialized city are convinced they're each other's problem, neither of them is looking at the people who de-industrialized the city or destabilized Somalia.

Building solidarity across national lines doesn't require ignoring the real difficulties of migration or the real strains on communities. It requires insisting that those difficulties be analyzed honestly — asking who benefits from the current arrangement and who pays for it, rather than accepting the framing that one group of workers is the threat to another group of workers.

**People move because they have to. They are not the cause of the conditions that made them move.**

The people who made those conditions? They have excellent passports. They never wait in line.
